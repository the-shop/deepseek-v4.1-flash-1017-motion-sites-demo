02 — Process

The drawing, line by line

Vael Tower

Study 04 — Isometric

Scale 1:200

01 — Ground Every project begins as a site survey: contours, sun paths, the neighbours' windows. We draw what is already there before adding a single line of our own.

02 — Structure A concrete frame spans seven metres in every direction. Nothing is hidden: the columns stay visible at every floor, so the building explains itself.

03 — Envelope Limestone, glass, and a deep reveal at each window. The facade is drawn twice — once for the street, once for the sky above it.

04 — Light Vertical slots cut through eight floors, dropping daylight into the plan. At noon the building holds no shadow at its centre.

Manifesto

We build slowly. Stone is quarried, not printed. Light is studied, not simulated. A room is finished only when it is quiet. Our practice is a long argument with gravity — and we are patient.

Written by the partners, Athens, 2023

04 — Approach

Three rules
we don't break.

They are not marketing. They are the sequence in which decisions get made, and they have cost us commissions we would have liked.

01

Draw the site first.

Before a single wall, we spend two weeks measuring what already exists: the slope, the prevailing wind, the shadow of the building next door. The first forty drawings of any Vael project are of something we did not make.

02

One material, used completely.

Every building is organised around a single primary material — limestone, board-formed concrete, cross-laminated timber. We would rather understand one thing deeply than specify twenty we only half know.

03

Leave the last ten percent empty.

Rooms need room. We keep a deliberate margin in every plan — a window seat, a stair that is wider than it must be — so the building can absorb a life it did not predict.
