01 — Why we exist

Every second breath you take is borrowed from the sea.

Marine phytoplankton produce roughly half of the oxygen in Earth's atmosphere. They also absorb carbon, feed the fish that feed a billion people, and help regulate the temperature of the entire planet. The ocean is not a backdrop to the climate crisis — it is the frontline.

Deep Blue Trust was founded in 2009 by four marine biologists and one very stubborn fisherwoman from Plymouth. Today we run 24 field sites across 11 countries, and not one of them is coastline-only.

50% of the oxygen you breathe is produced in the ocean
1.5°C of warming already locked into the upper ocean
8 Mt of plastic entering the sea every single year
91% of marine species remain undescribed by science

02 — Where we work

Four zones. One continuous lifeline.

The ocean is not one habitat. It is a stack of them, each with its own physics, its own species and its own rules. We work across all four.

01 0 – 200 m

The Sunlight Zone

Coral nurseries in Palau and the Florida Keys. 34,000 fragments replanted since 2019, with a 78% survival rate at twelve months.

02 200 – 1,000 m

The Twilight Zone

Acoustic monitoring of the deep scattering layer, where roughly 90% of ocean biomass migrates upward every night to feed.

03 1,000 – 4,000 m

The Midnight Zone

ROV surveys mapping cold-water coral gardens off the Azores — forests that took 8,000 years to grow and 20 minutes to damage.

04 4,000 – 6,000 m

The Abyss

A three-year campaign for a moratorium on deep-sea mining across 1.4 million km² of the Clarion-Clipperton Zone.

03 — Field work

Science first. Then noise.

Every campaign begins with data and ends with a policy document. Here is what five years of descents actually produced.

2019 Kuril Trench ROV survey, Northwest Pacific 11 species new to science
2021 Seagrass restoration, Ria Formosa, Portugal 12 hectares replanted · 4.3 kt CO₂/year
2022 Blue shark acoustic tagging, Azores 240 sharks tracked across 1.6M km²
2023 Community ranger patrols, Cabo Delgado 47 patrols · zero illegal longlines recorded
2024 Deep-sea mining moratorium campaign 14 governments signed the pledge
