[ 02 ]  The lab

We treat a model's internals as a scientific instrument — not a mystery to be averaged away.

Cortex is an independent research lab founded in 2019 by four researchers from interpretability and distributed systems. Sixty-two people across two cities work on a single question: can we build systems whose reasoning is legible to the people who depend on them?

We do not ship a model we cannot explain. That constraint is the whole lab.

[ 03 ]  Research

Four programs.
One question.

Each program runs its own compute budget and publishes independently. Findings cross over constantly — the interpretability team audits the sparse models, the alignment team builds evals against them.

01 09 papers

Mechanistic Interpretability

We reverse-engineer circuits inside transformer models — induction heads, attention paths, and the sparse features that carry meaning. The output is a map you can inspect, not a heatmap you have to trust.

Explore program
02 11 papers

Sparse Architectures

Mixture-of-experts routing that stays legible. Every token's path through the network is logged, attributed and replayable — so a 70B model can explain which 2B parameters actually fired.

Explore program
03 14 papers

Alignment & Control

Steering vectors, refusal geometry, and control protocols that survive scale. We measure what a model actually does under distribution shift — not what it says it would do.

Explore program
04 07 papers

Evaluation Science

Benchmarks that resist contamination. Held-out suites with cryptographic provenance, published before the models they test, so a score means something three years later.
